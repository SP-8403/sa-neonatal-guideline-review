HYPEROXIA TEST IN THE CYANOTIC NEWBORN

Use to support differentiation between cardiac and pulmonary causes of persistent central cyanosis

PROCEDURE

Purpose

The hyperoxia test assists in differentiating cardiac from pulmonary causes of cyanosis.

1

Administer oxygen

Administer 100% oxygen for 10 minutes.

2

Measure pre-ductal saturation

Measure oxygen saturation in the right hand.

3

Obtain arterial blood gas

Obtain arterial blood gas from the right radial artery to measure PaO₂.

4

Use chest x-ray selectively

Chest x-ray is not part of the hyperoxia test but may provide additional diagnostic information.

Important

Interpret results with the full clinical assessment, pre- and post-ductal saturations, perfusion, respiratory findings and haemodynamic stability.

ESCALATION AND SAFETY

UNSTABLE CYANOTIC NEONATE

Requires urgent referral irrespective of the hyperoxia test result.

Discuss urgently if cardiac disease is suspected

Urgently discuss with the referral centre or paediatric cardiology service if the clinical assessment or hyperoxia test suggests cyanotic congenital heart disease or a duct-dependent lesion.

Do not delay stabilisation

Continue airway and breathing support, pre-ductal saturation monitoring, glucose correction, temperature control and perfusion assessment while awaiting transfer.

Consider sepsis if clinically indicated

If cyanosis persists despite adequate ventilation and oxygenation, and the baby is unwell, take a blood culture where feasible and start broad-spectrum antibiotics according to local neonatal sepsis guidance.

Documentation

  • Record oxygen exposure time.
  • Record right-hand oxygen saturation.
  • Record arterial PaO₂ result and units.
  • Record referral discussion, advice and transfer plan.
Interpret the hyperoxia test with the full clinical assessment. Escalate early when cyanosis persists.
URGENT REFERRAL: Unstable cyanotic neonates require urgent discussion and transfer planning.
