## 2. Clinical Safety Review

### 🔴 2.1 Prostaglandin Administration  
**Priority: HIGH**

**Issue:** Oral prostaglandin referenced without IV hierarchy.

**Risk:** Misinterpretation in unstable neonates; potential delay of appropriate IV therapy.

**Required Action:**  
- IV Prostaglandin E1 must be first-line in suspected duct-dependent lesions.  
- Oral PGE2 only if IV access unavailable AND urgent transfer initiated.

---

### 🔴 2.2 Hyperoxia Test Interpretation  
**Priority: HIGH**

**Issue:** Thresholds overly academic and potentially unsafe for district use.

**Risk:** False reassurance; delayed referral.

**Required Action:** Simplify to operational interpretation:

- PaO2 >150 mmHg → Likely pulmonary  
- PaO2 <100 mmHg → Suspect congenital heart disease  
- Clinically unstable → Refer urgently regardless of result  

---

### 🟠 2.3 Cyanosis Definition  
**Priority: MEDIUM**

Central cyanosis must prompt urgent evaluation regardless of saturation threshold.

## 3. Governance and Escalation

### 🔴 3.1 Referral Pathways  
**Priority: HIGH**

Documented referral pathways must be mandatory, not advisory.

---

### 🔴 3.2 Escalation Triggers  
**Priority: HIGH**

Explicit urgent referral criteria must be listed for:
- Persistent central cyanosis  
- Pathological murmur  
- Shock  
- Abnormal four-limb BP  

---

### 🟠 3.3 Inotrope Use  
**Priority: MEDIUM**

Restrict initiation of advanced inotropes to monitored settings.
