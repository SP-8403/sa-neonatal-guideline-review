SVT Treatment in Neonates

Emergency response when supraventricular tachycardia is suspected

VERY RAPID / REGULAR / FIXED RATE
DETERMINE STABILITY AND TREAT SUSPECTED SVT

Suspect SVT

  • Very rapid, regular, fixed heart rate
  • P waves absent or difficult to see
  • Usually narrow QRS complex
  • May be associated with poor feeding, pallor, shock or cardiac failure

If baby unstable

Shock, hypotension, altered level of consciousness, respiratory distress, cardiac failure or clinical deterioration

  • Stabilise airway, breathing and circulation
  • Give oxygen and respiratory support as needed
  • Obtain intravenous (IV) or intraosseous (IO) access
  • Check glucose and correct hypoglycaemia
  • If available, obtain ECG rhythm strip or 12-lead ECG
  • Discuss urgently with referral centre or paediatric cardiology
  • Do not delay emergency treatment prior to transfer
  • Perform synchronised cardioversion if the baby remains unstable

If cardioversion is required

  • Use synchronised cardioversion
  • Initial energy: 0.5–1 J/kg
  • If ineffective, increase to 2 J/kg
  • Provide sedation or analgesia if possible, but do not delay cardioversion in an unstable baby

If baby stable

Well perfused, no signs of respiratory distress or cardiac failure

  • Continue cardiorespiratory and oxygen saturation monitoring
  • Obtain a 12-lead ECG if possible
  • Establish IV or IO access
  • Consider vagal manoeuvres
  • Prepare adenosine if SVT persists

Vagal manoeuvres

Stable baby only

  • A cold facial stimulus may be considered if appropriate expertise is available, using an ice-cold water-filled glove or wrapped cold pack applied briefly to the face while maintaining airway patency
  • Do not use ocular pressure, carotid sinus massage or abdominal pressure
  • Do not delay administation of adenosine if vagal manoeuvres are ineffective or urgent escalation if the baby becomes unstable

After conversion or failed response

  • Continue monitoring after rhythm conversion
  • Check glucose, electrolytes, calcium and magnesium
  • Discuss recurrent, resistant or uncertain rhythm with paediatric cardiology
  • Arrange admission or transfer according to local referral pathway

Adenosine dosing

First dose

0.1 mg/kg IV or IO

Give as a rapid push followed immediately by a normal saline flush

Second dose

0.2 mg/kg IV or IO

Repeat if first dose ineffective

  • Continuous ECG and cardiorespiratory monitoring
  • Obtain IV or IO access as close to the central circulation (heart) as possible e.g., antecubital fossa
  • Administer adenosine rapidly, followed immediately by a rapid saline flush
  • Maximum single dose: 0.3 mg/kg/dose
  • Adenosine has a short half-life and may be associated with transient facial flushing, bradycardia or asystole (rare)
  • Ensure resuscitation readiness before administration
  • Record rhythm response during administration
If rhythm diagnosis is uncertain, the baby is unstable, adenosine is ineffective, or broad-complex tachycardia is present, manage as an emergency and obtain urgent specialist input
